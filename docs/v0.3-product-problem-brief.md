# OT-RRDE v0.3: product problem brief

**23 November 2026 · Draft hypothesis · Offline research prototype**

## User and problem to test

The proposed user is an OT vulnerability analyst preparing a case for an operations or asset owner. A possible sponsor is the OT security lead. Initial discovery should focus on teams that already maintain vulnerability records and reconcile advisories, asset facts and change prerequisites.

**Hypothesis:** reconciling missing, conflicting or late evidence consumes material effort and can produce poorly justified investigation or remediation advice. The frequency, cost and importance of this problem remain unvalidated.

## Proposed smallest useful workflow

For one asset/vulnerability at a fixed decision time, import structured, permitted offline records and produce a reviewable decision note. It should show evidence origins and dates, supported claims, unresolved conflicts, investigation urgency, candidate next steps and outstanding operational prerequisites. Qualified humans retain change authority.

The published v0.2.1 engine provides the research baseline. Development code checks EPSS timing; broader source-conflict and correction handling remains proposed. A claimed control or approval is an input assertion requiring evidence. Initial evaluation uses synthetic dossiers.

## Existing approaches and possible contribution

Manual records, advisory review and change control are discovery comparators whose actual use must be established in interviews. [SSVC][1] already includes mission, safety, exposure and mitigation context. [Tenable][2] documents threat-informed OT prioritisation and remediation insights; [Claroty][3] describes contextual CPS exposure assessment and prioritisation.

**Potential contribution:** make the evidence available at a decision cutoff reproducible and its uncertainty easier to review. Public documentation establishes overlap; it does not establish that competing tools lack these capabilities. Novelty and comparative benefit remain open.

## Evidence before expanding the product

| Question | Planned evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Is the problem recurring and costly? | Three to five discovery conversations across at least two organisations. Record a recent non-confidential example, current alternatives, task frequency, preparation/review time, rework and decision ownership. These are recruitment targets, not proof of demand. |
| Does the policy improve decisions? | Follow the [validation protocol](v0.3-research-validation-plan.md): two independent scorers; 24 held-out vignettes in 12 case families; frozen cases, policies, adapters and scoring rules. Preserve disagreements and unscorable cases. |
| Is the comparison fair? | Address the [SSVC audit](v0.3-ssvc-pilot-mapping.md) finding of 0/8 complete input combinations through a dated dossier revision. Preserve the [original reviewer packet](v0.3-reviewer-pilot.md). Judge investigation urgency and deployment priority separately. |
| Is the workflow useful? | A separately agreed offline pilot comparing the participant's existing process with the proposed workflow. Measure total evidence-entry and review time, clarification requests and acceptable actions; vary method order across participants. |

Proposed research gates are zero unsafe proposals on explicit-blocker cases, zero post-cutoff influence on timing challenges and fewer unsupported-evidence decisions than v0.2.1, while preserving its escalation of jointly rated urgent cases. Report routine escalation, unresolved cases, coverage and raw denominators alongside any improvement. Small synthetic results do not establish field effectiveness.

## Next decision

Prepare an interview guide, seek independent criticism and arrange discovery conversations. Expand the workflow only if practitioners independently describe a shared unmet need and a suitable participant agrees measurable pilot objectives. Re-scope if current approaches already solve it or evidence-entry effort outweighs the benefit. Buyer, adoption requirements, pricing and willingness to pay remain unvalidated.

**Immediate interview prompt:** “Walk me through a recent vulnerability decision: which evidence was hardest to reconcile, what did you use, how much effort did it take, and who approved the next step?”
