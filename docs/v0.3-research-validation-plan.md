# OT-RRDE v0.3 research validation plan

**Draft protocol for technical criticism · 16 November 2026**

## Research question

When the same fictional OT case contains missing, conflicting or time-inconsistent evidence, does preserving the provenance of that evidence improve investigation and remediation advice over v0.2.1? The proposed benefit is fewer decisions driven by unusable evidence, with timely escalation of credible threats and useful coverage of routine cases.

The two decisions are **how soon a human should investigate** and **which next step is justified by the operational evidence**. Assess them independently. The unit of analysis is one affected component and fictional asset at a fixed decision time. Several snapshots or variants of the same underlying scenario form one case family.

## Baseline and first counterexample

The baseline is [commit fcfafc5](https://github.com/yassarsambo/ot-risk-remediation-decision-engine/commit/fcfafc5ab9cfd4c0f9ed14ef9787ea62d786cfd3) with [policy v0.2.1](../policy/v0.2.1.json) selected explicitly. The CLI defaults to v0.1. Python 3.12 reproduced the 40 tests and 15/15 author-defined regression matches. The challenge set yielded eight P1, three P2, four unresolved and zero P3 outcomes. The matches establish consistency with the published policy. Independent decision quality remains unmeasured.

One development probe already isolates a timing problem. Take a copy of `BENCH-014` with verified internet reachability, a synthetic severity of 9.6, `kev=false` and `active_exploitation_reported=false`. With EPSS set to 0.30, dating the score one day before the decision gives P1. Moving only the score date to one day after the decision still gives P1 and adds `THREAT_DATA_DATE_AFTER_RECORDING`. Removing the score gives P2. Use this probe during development to check that evidence dated after the decision stops influencing triage. Use fresh case families for the held-out test.

A second probe should preserve a useful baseline behaviour. With a positive exploitation signal and unresolved reachability, v0.2.1 emits `NEEDS_CONTEXT_REVIEW` with `URGENT_EXPERT_TRIAGE_FLAG_DESPITE_UNRESOLVED_CASE`. The evaluation should give the baseline credit for that flag and assess whether the human-facing explanation conveys the urgency.

## Evidence rules to test

Give each vignette a UTC decision cutoff. Record the source and record ID, source type, scope, claim and confidence basis, observation or score date, date available to the decision-maker, and any independently attributable origin or superseding correction. Include the snapshot or model version when one exists. Distinguish an unverified assertion from an assertion supported in the fictional dossier. All test dossiers and source records are synthetic.

| Evidence | Interpretation and treatment |
| --- | --- |
| **CISA KEV** | A match for the same CVE in a snapshot available at the cutoff supports exploitation in the wild. Record the catalogue version, snapshot availability and `dateAdded` separately. Listing date is administrative and asset applicability is a separate judgement. A CVE absent from a checked snapshot is distinct from an unchecked catalogue. Both can coexist with a credible external report. [CISA inclusion criteria](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCISA/bulletins/41cad06) · [Catalogue schema](https://github.com/cisagov/kev-data/blob/develop/known_exploited_vulnerabilities_schema.json) |
| **FIRST EPSS** | Treat the probability as a CVE-level estimate of observed exploitation activity over the next 30 days. Record the score date, model version and snapshot provenance. For each vignette, designate the latest daily edition due before the cutoff. Use a score from that edition if its source and availability are supported in the dossier. Mark the usable score as missing otherwise and retain old or future-dated inputs in the audit record. Missing differs from zero. A probability applies to the CVE-level forecast. Keep the baseline numerical cutoff of 0.10 to isolate the effect of provenance. [FIRST EPSS FAQ](https://www.first.org/epss/faq) · [FIRST historical data](https://www.first.org/epss/data) |
| **Reported exploitation** | Record the underlying advisory or observation, whether it describes actual exploitation or proof of concept, the affected scope, the dates and whether a later correction applies. Distinguish corroborated observation, credible but unresolved reporting, unsubstantiated claims, proof of concept alone, and evidence unavailable. Trace republished claims to their origin and count them once. Credible unresolved reports can warrant expedited human review with the uncertainty visible. |
| **Local controls and exposure** | Record which path and configuration the evidence covers and what the asserted verification actually showed. Treat contradictory evidence, evidence for a different path, and an intervening change that invalidates a verification separately from elapsed age. A relevant control can inform remediation feasibility and which facts to check next. It cannot by itself suppress an independent exploitation escalation. |

Resolve apparent contradictions at the level of the claim. A high EPSS score and an empty KEV lookup answer different questions. A vendor statement about an unaffected firmware version and a positive KEV match can both be true. If two applicable reports disagree about exposure or a control, retain the disagreement and name the evidence a reviewer should obtain. A confirmed threat and unresolved local applicability can require urgent investigation while the patch decision awaits more evidence.

## Cases and independent review

Use the 15 existing cases for regression and up to **eight new pilot cases** for the input specification, reviewer rubric and comparator adapters. Freeze the hold-out design at **24 new vignettes across 12 matched pairs**. Keep each family entirely within one split, including variants of the same asset with a changed date or one changed fact. Target at least six plausible routine cases at design time, then report how many reviewers actually judge routine. Across the suite, use fictional examples from several OT settings and balance change-sensitive control equipment with lower-consequence monitoring assets.

| Held-out stratum | Cases | Example contrasts |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Routine and near routine | 4 | Low-consequence, sufficiently evidenced cases with a routine next step. Test routine coverage across different change-readiness states. |
| Threat provenance and timing | 4 | In-time versus post-cutoff intelligence. Shared-origin reports versus independently attributable evidence. |
| Active threat and local uncertainty | 4 | Known exploitation with independently supported versus uncertain reachability. Evidence of exploitation versus proof of concept or prediction alone. |
| Control scope and freshness | 4 | Otherwise equivalent verification straddling the 90-day threshold. Recent verification with and without an intervening configuration change that invalidates it. Include evidence for a different path in the local-uncertainty stratum. |
| Operational preconditions | 4 | High availability with approved versus missing operations review. Known nonredundancy with an approved contingency versus unresolved redundancy. Include a legitimate conditional patch proposal as a positive check. |
| Applicability and source conflict | 4 | An applicable routine finding versus a credible disagreement about the affected version. Repeat with a second low-consequence family and a distinct evidence gap. |

Prepare a short methods brief using the existing v0.2 reviewer questions on counterexamples, operational realism and competing approaches. A methods critic can challenge the protocol without joining the scoring panel. Seek two independent scorers with complementary experience in OT vulnerability management and operations or safety engineering. Invite a methods researcher to critique the comparison and analysis. Have a reviewer contribute previously unseen counterexamples where practical. Record who authored the cases and what the scorers saw.

Give each scorer the same vignettes in random order with the source evidence visible. Keep policy outputs, expected labels, age thresholds and pair membership hidden until their judgements are locked. Ask each scorer for the following.

1. Human investigation timing using **immediate** (start triage now), **prompt** (assess ahead of the routine queue), **scheduled** (review on the normal cadence) or **insufficient context**. Use a separate flag for cases that require immediate attention while facts are gathered.
2. Acceptable next steps. Permit multiple selections from gather evidence and escalate, plan controlled mitigation and patch review, or make a conditional patch proposal with named prerequisites. Identify explicitly unsafe steps.
3. The decisive evidence, any blocker and the evidence request that could resolve it.
4. Whether the facts are complete enough to judge each decision.
5. Deployment priority when the vignette specifies an applicable and deployable patch. Use **defer**, **scheduled**, **out of cycle** or **immediate** under the published Deployer definitions. Mark cases outside that scope as such. Record this independently of the investigation timing.

Report initial independent agreement by endpoint and retain both rationales. For priority, use the agreed timing or urgency flag when both scorers agree. For actions, use the intersection of their acceptable sets when nonempty. Keep disagreements and insufficient-context cases in the report with reasons. A third OT-qualified reviewer can assess a material disagreement separately. Report any adjudication alongside the original labels. A single scorer provides pilot evidence. Agree with scorers on how their ratings may be used and get separate permission for public attribution.

## Comparisons and analysis

| Comparison | Use |
| --- | --- |
| **Published v0.2.1** | Primary paired comparison for urgency, evidence use and candidate actions. Project the same vignette into the legacy fields using a documented adapter. Preserve available legacy claims and dates and record information lost in projection. Feed the candidate and the baseline the same facts and the same deliberately questionable records. |
| **CERT/CC SSVC Deployer** | External comparison for relative priority. Use the published `ssvc:DT_DP:1.0.0` table with Exploitation, System Exposure, Automatable and Human Impact. Map these using their published definitions and the shared vignette facts, including mission and safety impact. Record uncertain inputs as unscorable and verify the adapter against the published rows. Score each model against the corresponding human priority judgement. Compare their orderings directly on scorable pairs where the two reference priorities agree on the order. Show cases where investigation and deployment priorities diverge, plus ties and coverage. Score patch readiness against the human action rubric and v0.2.1. [Deployer definitions](https://certcc.github.io/SSVC/howto/deployer_tree/) · [Pinned decision table](https://github.com/CERTCC/SSVC/blob/bf3213bc0bd2bf3cf1329c9b48f72d7163a682b1/data/json/decision_tables/ssvc/deployer_patch_application_priority_1_0_0.json) |
| **Synthetic severity alone** | Retain descending severity-proxy order as a continuity check for relative priority. Treat equal scores as ties. The proxy values are fictional and provide no CVSS measurements. |

Run the full candidate and two single-change ablations. In the first, bypass the new threat-provenance checks using the frozen legacy projection and keep the operational and control checks. In the second, disable only the control age cutoff and preserve checks for missing dates, future dates, control relevance and supersession. Sweep control age limits of **30, 60, 90 and 180 days**. Use a development grid at each threshold minus one day, at the threshold and plus one day. Define the boundary as inclusive. Vary evidence ages across eligible held-out dossiers so the sweep has meaningful contrasts. Report which outputs and reasons move and why. Treat the age values as experimental settings and report sensitivity without tuning the chosen policy to the held-out labels.

| Endpoint | Scoring |
| --- | --- |
| **Unsafe candidate actions** | Count recommendations that present a patch as feasible despite an explicit blocking readiness or availability fact, or that omit a required independent safety gate. Score the action with its stated conditions. A proposal expressly conditional on a named engineering approval is judged with that condition. Report the denominator of applicable challenge cases. |
| **Unsupported evidence use** | Count whether evidence unavailable at the cutoff changed investigation priority or remediation advice, and whether a control was credited despite a clear scope conflict or invalidating change. Score explicit evidence failures against the dossier and report disputed cases separately. |
| **Urgent misses and routine burden** | Count missed immediate escalation among jointly rated urgent cases. Count expedited outcomes and unresolved decisions separately among jointly rated routine cases. Treat the baseline urgent flag on an unresolved case as expedited. Report whether each method surfaces the reason in its explanation. |
| **Acceptable actions and priority agreement** | Count membership in the shared acceptable action set and order agreement on jointly judged comparable pairs. Report justified conditional patch proposals, unnecessary deferrals, unscorable cases and disagreements. Use the same scorable pairs for direct method comparisons and publish each method's overall coverage. |

Report paired changes with counts, denominators and case family IDs. Show safety-critical cases and reviewer disagreements individually. The cohort is small, intentionally selected and clustered by design. Treat rates as descriptive sample results and give the reader the raw denominators.

## What would warrant a research claim

Before scoring, timestamp a manifest with the source and policy commits, case split and hashes, adapters, rubric, scoring rules and planned analyses. Log protocol amendments with their dates and reasons. Freeze the candidate before seeing hold-out expectations or outputs. Report the original experiment if a failure prompts a revision and reserve new case families for the revised candidate's next held-out test.

The proposed first-pass criteria are zero unsafe actions among cases with explicit blockers, zero decisions influenced by post-cutoff evidence on the timing challenges, and fewer unsupported evidence decisions than v0.2.1. Check that the candidate still expedites every jointly rated urgent case that the baseline expedites. Report the trade-off in routine escalation, unresolved cases and acceptable action coverage even when the critical checks pass. If the sample contains no affected cases or too few agreed urgency judgements to test the criteria, report that endpoint as inconclusive. Negative or mixed results are suitable to share with their limitations.

Prior work already covers risk-based OT prioritisation and the importance of testing, safety and availability in patching. The contribution to investigate is a **reproducible, independently assessed experiment** on evidence provenance, uncertainty and decision timing. Compare that specific claim with at least the following sources and record which design elements they already cover.

- NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 for OT safety, reliability and patch testing. [NIST guide](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/82/r3/final)
- RITICS-hosted guidance for ICS/OT vulnerability management, contextual prioritisation and scheduled downtime. [Vulnerability Management in ICS/OT Environments](https://ritics.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Vulnerability-Management-in-ICS-OT-Environments.pdf)
- CERT/CC SSVC for stakeholder-specific priority and explicit human impact. [SSVC Deployer model](https://certcc.github.io/SSVC/howto/deployer_tree/)
- SmartPatch for published patch prioritisation research on interdependent systems. Use the full paper in the related-work matrix before making novelty claims about its methods. [SmartPatch paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compind.2021.103595)

## Delivery order

1. **Case specification and pilot.** Define the source fields, decision cutoff and legacy adapter. Turn the future-dated EPSS probe into the first regression test for the candidate. Draft the pilot vignettes and one-page annotation rubric. Ask a methods critic to challenge whether this establishes a meaningful difference from existing OT practice.
2. **Build only the comparison.** Add the candidate checks and offline experiment runner on a development branch. Run the legacy suite, paired invariants and comparator checks. Freeze the protocol and collect independent judgements.
3. **Report the experiment.** Ship a replay manifest, the permitted synthetic vignettes and scoring data, case-level error analysis and a short reproducibility report. Synthetic findings are evidence about the tested decision rule under specified conditions. Field effectiveness, safety assurance, adoption and economic impact require separate evidence. Consider a methods workshop such as CSET if the experiment contributes a transferable evaluation method. [CSET scope](https://cset26.isi.edu/cfp.html)
4. **Revisit commercial work when there is evidence.** Look for independent practitioners to identify a recurring evidence-reconciliation problem in their current workflow and a specific advantage over their existing triage and change-control process. Establish the research contribution and the workflow need before assessing willingness to adopt or pay.

Reproduce the published baseline with `python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v` and `python3 scripts/evaluate_benchmark.py` from the repository root. The evaluator selects v0.2.1 explicitly. For future runs, record the interpreter version, commit SHA, case and policy hashes, and the exact commands alongside outputs.
